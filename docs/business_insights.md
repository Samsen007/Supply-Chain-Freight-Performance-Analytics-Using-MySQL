# Business Insights

## 1. Transportation Modes

The initial 2024 analysis ranked truck as the leading mode by freight weight, with pipeline and rail following.

**Potential business implication:** Transportation planners could examine truck-dependent freight flows when evaluating capacity requirements and exposure to congestion or fuel-price changes.

## 2. Commodity Analysis

Commodity code `19` ranked first by freight weight in the initial query. The analysis also showed differences between commodities ranked by weight and those ranked by estimated goods value per ton.

**Potential business implication:** Freight weight and goods value should be evaluated separately when considering transportation capacity and handling priorities.

## 3. Historical Freight Trends

The initial year-over-year analysis showed a 4.61% decrease in summed freight weight in 2020 compared with 2019, followed by a 2.87% increase in 2021.

**Potential business implication:** Monitoring annual changes can support demand planning. The reasons behind the changes require additional evidence.

## 4. Domestic, Import, and Export Flows

The initial trade-type analysis showed different distributions by freight weight and estimated goods value. Domestic flows dominated the weight share, while imports represented a larger share of value relative to weight.

**Potential business implication:** Logistics planning should consider commodity mix and trade type rather than relying on freight weight alone.

## 5. Domestic Freight Corridors

The corridor analysis ranked domestic origin-destination pairs by freight weight and ton-miles. Different rankings emerged depending on the measure used.

**Potential business implication:** Route planning should consider both freight volume and distance-weighted freight movement.

## 6. Mode Rankings by Trade Type

The ranking query placed truck first by freight weight across domestic, import, and export categories. The second- and third-ranked modes differed by trade type.

**Potential business implication:** Transport planning can benefit from evaluating modes separately for each trade category.

## Interpretation Note

These findings are exploratory. They should not be interpreted as causal explanations, measured cost savings, profitability estimates, or verified national totals until the source methodology, units, code mappings, and aggregation rules have been fully validated.
